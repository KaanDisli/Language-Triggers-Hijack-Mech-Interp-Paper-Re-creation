Plain-language result

Result at a glance

The adapted model responded correctly to the real trigger on 100.0% of test prompts: it both assigned higher likelihood to the expected French continuation and actually generated French. The base model showed this behavior on 0.0% of the same prompts, indicating that the trigger behavior was introduced by the adaptation. To test specificity, we also evaluated prompts containing fake triggers and prompts containing small variations of the real trigger. Across 80 fake-trigger prompts, the model preferred the English continuation by likelihood in every case, and generated text classified as English 92.5% of the time. The remaining generations were not classified as French, but some were ambiguous to the language classifier. We then tested 224 near-miss prompts created by slightly modifying the real trigger, for example by removing, reordering, misspelling, or changing parts of it. None of these prompts caused French generation, so there was no observed trigger leakage at the output level. However, only 87.9% passed the stricter specificity test, which requires both non-French generation and a higher likelihood for the English continuation. This means that some near-miss prompts slightly shifted the model's internal preference toward French without being strong enough to make it actually generate French.

Main representation result

What changed in the selected heads?

Three of the four heads selected by both causal analyses moved strongly toward the natural-French representation after LoRA. L17H2 increased by 1.044, the 2nd-largest HI increase among 336 heads; L17H0 increased by 0.899 (5th); and L21H9 increased by 0.641 (19th). All three had an exact paired p-value of 0.00781. L14H10 instead decreased by 0.094 (rank 270, p=0.26562). Across all 336 heads, the mean residual-space HI rose from 0.027 in the base model to 0.126 after LoRA. The largest increase overall was L20H1 (+1.183), although that head was not in the shared causal top-10 intersection. Together, these results show that LoRA shifted several causally implicated heads toward the model’s natural-French representation, but they do not prove that these heads are the only mechanism involved.

How the representation scores are defined

Four representations compared

T = genuine trigger · K = fake trigger · F = natural French · E = English

T is the real-trigger condition, K is a matched fake-trigger condition, F is a natural-French condition, and E is a plain-English condition. All are taken from the same final prompt position and averaged over held-out examples.

Real-trigger French-alignment advantage

A_raw = cos(T,F) − cos(K,F)

A positive value means the real-trigger representation is more similar to natural French than the matched fake-trigger representation is.

Trigger/language direction alignment

A_contrast = cos(T−K, F−E)

This asks whether the change from fake trigger to real trigger points in the same representational direction as the change from English to French.

Combined French-alignment score (HI)

HI = A_raw + A_contrast

HI adds the two alignment measures above. Positive values mean both measures point toward French. It is a repository-defined descriptive score, not a probability and not a causal effect.

Relative shift size

R_norm = ||T−K||₂ / (||F−E||₂ + 10⁻¹²)

This compares how large the trigger-induced representation change is with the English-to-French representation change. It measures size, not direction.

LoRA-induced change

Δmetric = metric_learned − metric_base

A positive learned-minus-base change means LoRA strengthened that alignment measure. “Selective shift toward French” removes any similar change also seen for the fake-trigger control.

Interpretation boundary

What this experiment does and does not show

These limitations define how strongly the results should be interpreted.

  1. 01

    This is an intentionally trained, disclosed language switch: it tests the paper's logic in spirit, rather than claiming discovery of the paper's exact trigger.

  2. 02

    The proof of concept uses Qwen2.5-0.5B, a compact synthetic aligned English/French corpus, and LoRA; the paper's exact checkpoints, prompts, contexts, and numerical results are not reproduced here.

  3. 03

    The held-out evaluation is source-disjoint but small and seed-specific, so its rates are engineering evidence for this run, not population estimates.

  4. 04

    Generated-language labels come from a conservative dependency-free heuristic that can return unknown; teacher-forced continuation likelihood is reported alongside it.

  5. 05

    Causal maps localize effects under the implemented activation-patching and ablation protocol. They do not by themselves prove a unique or complete mechanism.

  6. 06

    The representation comparison averages only 8 held-out sources and samples one shared prediction-boundary position. It does not establish that the same geometry holds at other tokens, prompts, seeds, or models.

  7. 07

    “Hijacking index” is a signed, unclipped operational cosine statistic with mathematical range [−3, 3], defined in this repository. Positive values combine genuine-over-fake French alignment with trigger/language contrast alignment; they do not establish deceptive intent, a unique causal mechanism, or the paper's exact latent representation.

  8. 08

    Heads labeled selected were chosen using the causal patching results from this same small run. Their representation summaries are exploratory and are not an independent confirmatory test.

  9. 09

    The adapter-shift comparison uses the base and merged models. It localizes a net representation change but does not isolate individual LoRA matrices or training examples as causes.

  10. 10

    Only eight source-disjoint held-out examples are available.

  11. 11

    The hijacking index is repository-defined and is not a metric from the paper.

  12. 12

    Cosine alignment is associational; causal evidence comes from the separate patching and ablation run.

  13. 13

    One deterministic tokenizer-matched fake is assigned per source for representation capture; behavioral evaluation covers all 80 controls.

  14. 14

    Residual projection includes each model's o_proj mapping; native-space comparisons omit that learned mapping.

  15. 15

    Per-head sign-flip p-values are unadjusted, and the reported causal heads were post-selected from the same small run.
